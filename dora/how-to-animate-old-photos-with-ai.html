How to animate old family photos with AI (2026)

Scan the photo, ask for one small movement, and check the face. How to prepare an old photo, how to word the prompt, what goes wrong and which tools can do it.

Maruta G · 25 November 2026 · Leer en español

Short answer: scan or photograph the print as cleanly as you can, give it to an image-to-video tool, and ask for one small, natural movement, such as a slow blink and a faint smile, for four to eight seconds. Then compare the face with the original. Faces are where this goes wrong, so check them first. Options include Dora (paid), Runway (a free plan of 125 one-time credits), MyHeritage Deep Nostalgia (faces only) and Google Photos (US only when announced).

Step by step

  1. Scan the photo well. A flatbed scan is best for prints; our rule of thumb is 600 dpi for a small print. With a phone, shoot flat and square to the print, in daylight, without flash, and crop to the photo's edge.
  2. Clean it first. Scratches, creases and stains tend to be animated as moving marks. Light cleanup before animating gives the tool less to misread.
  3. Pick a tool that starts from a photo. It must take an image as the first frame. See the options below.
  4. Write one small movement. Who moves, what they do, and what the camera does: "she turns her head slightly and smiles, fixed camera".
  5. Keep the clip short and check the face. Four to eight seconds. Put the original next to the result and look at eyes, teeth and the shape of the face.
  6. Keep the original and say it is animated. Share the clip as an AI animation, not as old footage.

Scan quality

The tool only sees the pixels you give it. Dora's Seedance 2 models output up to 720p (its models page lists no 1080p for them), so a scan of 1,500 to 2,000 pixels on the long side is plenty; a sharp, evenly lit scan matters more than a huge one. Practical points:

  • Avoid glare. Photographing a glossy print under a lamp leaves a bright patch that the tool may treat as part of the picture.
  • Straighten and crop. A white border or a table edge in the frame can end up moving or being filled in.
  • Do not over-sharpen. Sharpening filters add halos around faces that the video then exaggerates.
  • One face is easier than twenty. For a group portrait, consider cropping to one or two people and animating each separately.
  • Black and white is fine. Say in the prompt that it should stay black and white, or the tool may add colour.

Writing the movement

These are examples of wording. We did not generate them for this page, so we make no claim about how any model renders them.

She turns her head slightly toward the camera, blinks slowly and gives a faint smile, fixed camera, keep the original black and white photograph look
The man nods and adjusts his hat, a light breeze moves the curtain behind him, fixed camera, keep the faded film grain
The family stays in place, two of the children smile and one waves, fixed camera, no one walks out of the frame
  • Name who moves. "The woman on the left" is clearer than "she" in a group.
  • Ask for less than you want. Blink, nod, smile and a breeze are safer than "walks toward the camera and laughs".
  • Say what stays still. "Fixed camera" and "the background does not change" reduce drifting.
  • Keep the look. Repeating "black and white" or "faded colour film" keeps the clip matched to the photo.

What goes wrong with faces

  • The face drifts. After a second or two the person can look like a relative, or a stranger. This is the most common failure with any AI video model; shorter clips and smaller movements help.
  • Teeth and eyes. A closed-mouth portrait may open its mouth and show teeth that were never in the photo. Eyes can flicker or change shape.
  • Hands and glasses. Fingers can fuse or multiply, and glasses can warp.
  • Small faces in a group. People at the back have few pixels, and their faces tend to melt or swap features.
  • Added colour or sound. Some tools colourise a black and white photo, and some models add a soundtrack by default. Dora's Seedance 2 Mini makes sound by default and you can turn it off.

What helps: crop to fewer faces, ask for a smaller movement, make the clip shorter, and regenerate rather than fixing the same result. Generate a few versions and keep the one whose face is closest to the original. If the person is a relative, show the result to someone who knew them before you share it widely.

Animating an old photo in Dora

On the web, open Create. Seedance 2 Mini is already chosen because it is the default video model: add your photo as the first frame, write what should happen, choose the shape and the length (4, 8 or 12 seconds, at 480p or 720p), and generate. On the iPhone, tap Generate in the middle of the tab bar, then the AI Model card. On Android, the Google Play listing describes templates that put your photo into a video, and a Create screen where you describe your own idea, add one photo, and leave the model on Auto or pick one.

Dora's own notes on Seedance 2 Mini give two limits that matter here: it takes one reference photo, and 480p looks soft on large screens, so use 720p for the final version. The cost is shown before you generate, and if a generation fails the coins come back.

Questions people ask

Can AI animate an old family photo?

Yes. Tools that turn a still image into a short video can make a person blink, smile or turn their head. Results vary, and faces are the weak point: they can drift away from the person in the photo.

What is the best way to scan an old photo for animation?

A flatbed scan of the print, flat, evenly lit and cropped to the photo's edge, with no glare and no heavy sharpening. For a phone, shoot square to the print in daylight without flash. A clean image matters more than a very large one.

How do I write a prompt to animate an old photo?

Name who moves, ask for one small movement, say the camera stays fixed, and repeat the look of the photo, for example 'she turns her head slightly and smiles, fixed camera, keep the black and white look'.

Why does the face change in the video?

Image-to-video models rebuild the face frame by frame and can drift from the original, especially with large movements, long clips and small faces in groups. Use shorter clips, smaller movements and fewer faces, and compare with the original.

Is there a free way to animate an old photo?

Runway has a free plan with 125 one-time credits, per its pricing page on 25 November 2026. Google announced Photo to video in Google Photos for the U.S. in July 2025, with usage limits, but its post does not say whether it is free and we did not check current availability. Dora has no free generations.

Does Dora animate old photos?

Yes, on the web, iPhone and Android, from one photo. It is a paid service with coins shown before you generate, and it cannot guarantee that a face stays faithful to the original.
