How to help a baby sleep with white noise: volume, distance and timer (2026)

A steady sound can help some babies settle. The published advice is about doing it quietly, far from the cot and not all night by default. Ask your paediatrician about anything specific to your baby.

Maruta G · 23 October 2026 · Leer en español

We make Drop, a sleep sounds app, and it is one of the options below. This page repeats published guidance and does not add medical advice of its own.

What the research found

  • It can help some newborns settle. In a small randomised trial in London (Spencer et al., Archives of Disease in Childhood, 1990), 16 of 20 newborns (80%) fell asleep within five minutes with white noise, against 5 of 20 (25%) without it. The authors' conclusion is cautious: white noise “may help mothers settle difficult babies”.
  • Machines can be too loud. Researchers in Toronto measured 14 infant sleep machines at maximum volume, adjusting for a 6-month-old's ear canal (Hugh et al., Pediatrics, 2014). At 30 cm all of them were above 50 dBA, the recommended noise limit for hospital nurseries, and three were above 85 dBA, which over 8 hours exceeds adult workplace limits.
  • Distance and volume matter most. Writing about that study, a paediatrician at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia advises to “place them more than 200 centimeters from the crib” and “keep the volume low” (CHOP, 6 April 2014).
  • For adults the evidence is weak. A 2021 review of 38 studies rated the evidence that continuous noise improves sleep as very low quality (Riedy et al., 2021).

Safe sleep comes first

The American Academy of Pediatrics' safe sleep advice (HealthyChildren.org, updated 11 September 2026) says to place a baby on their back for every sleep, on a firm, flat surface, with “a fitted sheet only — nothing else should be in the crib”, sharing the room but not the bed for at least the first 6 months. That rules out a phone, speaker or sound machine in the cot or tied to its rails.

What we checked in Drop's code

We read Drop's sleep timer in the app's source code (SleepTimer): the options are 15, 30, 45 and 60 minutes, 2 hours, 8 hours, or no end, and in the last 30 seconds the volume falls gradually to silence instead of cutting off. Playback is set to continue with the screen locked.

Step by step

  1. Choose one steady sound. White noise, a fan, rain or a heartbeat. Pick one that is even and without sudden peaks, so it does not startle.
  2. Put the phone or speaker far from the cot. More than 200 cm away, never in the cot or on its rails, per the guidance above.
  3. Start low. Set the volume at the lowest level that covers the household noise. Listen from where the baby's head is: it should sound quieter than a normal conversation.
  4. Set a timer. Let the sound stop after the baby is asleep unless you have decided otherwise. On iPhone, Background Sounds has Stop Sound with a Timer (Apple); apps such as Drop have their own timer.
  5. Use Do Not Disturb or airplane mode. So a call or notification does not play through the same speaker at full volume. Sounds already downloaded keep playing.
  6. Check with your paediatrician. Especially for premature babies, or if your baby has hearing or ear problems.

Questions people ask

Is white noise safe for babies?

The published concern is volume and distance. A 2014 Pediatrics study found 14 infant sleep machines were all above 50 dBA at 30 cm at full volume. Keep the device more than 200 cm from the cot, at low volume, and ask your paediatrician.

Does white noise help babies sleep?

In a small 1990 trial, 80% of newborns fell asleep within five minutes with white noise versus 25% without. It is one small study; it may help some babies settle.

How loud should white noise be for a baby?

Low. The 2014 study used 50 dBA, the hospital nursery limit, as the reference. In practice, quieter than normal conversation where the baby's head is.

How far should a sound machine be from the crib?

More than 200 cm, according to the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, and never in the crib or on its rails.

Should white noise play all night?

The guidance is to keep exposure low; a timer that stops the sound after the baby falls asleep is the cautious default.

Can I use my iPhone as a white noise machine?

Yes: Background Sounds in iOS or a sleep sound app. Put the phone far from the cot, use Do Not Disturb, and set a timer.
