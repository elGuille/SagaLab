Pomodoro timer on iPhone: how to do it with the Clock app, Focus or an app (2026)

You do not need an app for Pomodoro: your iPhone's Clock and Focus already do the two things it needs. An app helps when you want the timer tied to the task, or a record of the time.

Maruta G · 23 October 2026, updated 24 October 2026 · Leer en español

We make 3 Tasks, one of the apps on this page. The built-in way comes first and is enough for many people.

Pomodoro in one paragraph

Francesco Cirillo created the Pomodoro Technique in the 1980s. In its usual form you work on one task for 25 minutes, take a short break of about 5 minutes, and take a longer break after four rounds. His own site stresses that it is more than a timer: you choose the day's work first, handle interruptions and look back at how long things took.

What the iPhone already does (Apple's guide, read 23 October 2026)

  • Timer in Clock. Set a duration, give it a label such as "Pomodoro" and pick the sound for when it ends. "The timer continues even if you open another app or if iPhone goes to sleep." A custom timer then appears under Recent, so the next 25 minutes is one tap (Apple).
  • Several timers at once. After starting one you can add more, from presets or recent ones, so a 25-minute work timer and a 5-minute break timer can sit side by side.
  • Siri. "Set the timer for 25 minutes."
  • Lock Screen. When a timer ends you get a notification and can tap Repeat or Stop from the Lock Screen.
  • Focus. A Focus such as Work silences all notifications or lets only some people and apps through, and can turn on automatically at a set time, place or "while using a certain app" (Apple).

What we checked in 3 Tasks' code

We read the timer and reminder code of the version on sale today (1.3.0). Each of the three tasks has a start/pause timer that counts up and keeps counting in the background. You can give a task a time limit of 15, 25, 45 or 60 minutes: a ring fills towards it, and when it is reached a notification says "Time's up" with the task's name, even with the app closed. There is no break timer: for the 5-minute break, use Clock. Since version 1.4.1 (24 October 2026) you can start a task's timer straight from the Home Screen widget: tap the task, in any widget size. There is no Siri command for the timer.

Step by step

  1. Choose the task first. Pomodoro works on one task at a time. Write it down, or pick one of your three for the day.
  2. Turn on a Focus. Control Center, Focus, then Do Not Disturb or Work. In Settings, Focus, you can make Work turn on by itself when you open the app you work in.
  3. Start a 25-minute timer. Clock, Timers, 25 minutes, label it "Pomodoro", Start; or say "Set the timer for 25 minutes". In 3 Tasks, set the task's limit to 25 min and tap the task.
  4. Work only on that task until it rings. If something else comes up, note it on paper and return to the task.
  5. Take a 5-minute break. Start a 5-minute timer from Presets or Recent in Clock. Stand up, look away from the screen.
  6. After four rounds, take a longer break. Fifteen to thirty minutes is the usual range.
  7. Look at the time you actually spent. Compare it with your guess. Apps that keep a history make this easier; on paper, a tick per round is enough.

Questions people ask

Does the iPhone have a Pomodoro timer?

Not by that name, but the Clock app's Timer does the job: set 25 minutes, label it, and start it again from Recent. You can run a work timer and a break timer at the same time.

Does the iPhone timer keep running if I lock the phone?

Yes. Apple says the timer continues if you open another app or iPhone goes to sleep, and you can Repeat or Stop it from the Lock Screen.

How long is a Pomodoro?

Usually 25 minutes of work and a short break of about 5 minutes, with a longer break after four rounds.

How do I block distractions during a Pomodoro?

Turn on a Focus such as Do Not Disturb or Work. A Focus can also turn on by itself when you open a certain app.

Can 3 Tasks do Pomodoro?

It times each task and can notify you after 15, 25, 45 or 60 minutes, but it has no break timer. Use Clock for the breaks, or a dedicated Pomodoro app if you want breaks counted for you.

Can I start the 3 Tasks timer from the widget?

Yes, since version 1.4.1 (24 October 2026): tap a task in the Home Screen widget, in any size, and its timer starts.
