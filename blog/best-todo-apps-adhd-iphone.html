Best to-do list apps for ADHD on iPhone (2026)

Five apps compared on how many decisions they ask of you: a timeline, step-by-step breakdown, timers or just three tasks. No app treats ADHD.

Maruta G · 25 November 2026 · Español

Short answer: no app is a treatment for ADHD, and this page is not medical advice. Among apps that describe themselves as made for ADHD, Tiimo and Structured both show your day as a visual timeline, and Goblin Tools breaks one task into steps. If a long list is what overwhelms you, 3 Tasks allows only three tasks a day. Todoist is a general-purpose tool with many options and no ADHD wording in its description. One of these apps is ours, and the disclosure is at the end. Prices and details are from the US App Store listings on 25 November 2026.

The National Institute of Mental Health describes ADHD as a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity, with inattention including difficulty "paying attention, keeping on task, or staying organized." It lists medication and psychosocial interventions as standard treatments. A to-do app is neither; at most it is a tool for organising a day. If you think you have ADHD, talk to a clinician.

Why fewer choices can help

This is the reasoning behind these designs, not a clinical finding, and we cite no study that it improves symptoms. A long list asks you to rank every item each time you open it. Apps that show one day on a timeline, break a task into steps or cap the list at three remove part of that ranking. Whether that helps you is something to test for a week, not assume.

1. Tiimo: the planner that describes itself as neurodivergent-friendly

Your day appears as a colour-coded visual timeline with drag-and-drop tasks, a focus countdown timer, and widgets for key tasks and timers on the home or lock screen. An AI Co-Planner helps create, prioritise and plan tasks, and a "Snap to plans" feature turns a photo of a paper list into tasks. The free version has a basic planner, limited AI chats and one profile. Its page says it is co-designed with ADHD and autism experts and has a neurodiversity award; those are the developer's statements.

Where others beat it: the AI features are limited on the free plan, and the listing shows its Pro prices without saying which is monthly or yearly.

2. Structured: a timeline with a free sub-task and timer set

It shows tasks, events and routines in order on a timeline. In the free version it includes an inbox, sub-tasks with notes, a Pomodoro focus timer, Live Activities and interactive widgets. Pro, sold monthly, yearly or as a lifetime purchase, adds calendar and Reminders connection, recurring tasks, AI day planning and replanning of missed tasks. Its page says it is built for ADHD minds, busy professionals and students.

Where others beat it: recurring tasks and calendar connection need Pro.

3. Goblin Tools: break a task into steps

Not a planner but a set of tools: Magic ToDo breaks overwhelming tasks into bite-sized steps, Estimator gives rough time estimates, Compiler turns a messy brain dump into an organised list of actions, and Formalizer and Judge rewrite or read the tone of a message. The app costs $0.99; a Pro plan adds syncing across devices.

Where others beat it: it does not keep your day: there is no timeline, widget or timer in its listing, so you would pair it with another app.

4. 3 Tasks: three a day, one timer each

Each day you write three tasks and work through them one at a time, with a start and pause timer on every task, Pomodoro style. The timer keeps counting with the screen locked, a task you do daily can repeat each morning, and there is a Home Screen widget, dark mode and a small celebration on finishing a task. All of that is free, with no ads and no account. Pro is a one-time purchase with no subscription and adds history, streaks, CSV export, extra themes, and in recent versions an Apple Watch app and today's calendar events as tasks. It sends one reminder if you have not opened it in two days.

Where others beat it: its listing describes no sub-tasks, no breaking a task into steps and no timeline, and it has no ADHD wording or advice; the limit of three is the whole idea. For a calendar view or many projects, Structured and Todoist do more.

5. Todoist: the general-purpose option

Projects, sub-tasks and sections, list, board and calendar views, recurring tasks, location reminders, voice entry and an Apple Watch app. It is a powerful system, and that is the point against it here: more options means more to configure. Its page says "focus on doing, not configuring".

Where others beat it: no ADHD wording in its description, and no timer.

How to choose

  • You want the whole day laid out visually: Tiimo or Structured.
  • You freeze when a task is big: Goblin Tools, next to any planner.
  • You want to decide only three things a day: 3 Tasks. If you want other ways to do that, see our ranking of to-do apps that limit you to 3 tasks a day.
  • You already run projects and want one system: Todoist.
