Best AI apps for confidential documents on iPhone (2026)

For confidential PDFs on iPhone, Noema and On-Device AI do the most with documents. Pinku is ours, third, paid, and has no online option in the app.

Maruta G · 24 November 2026

Written from App Store pages on 24 November 2026. We read the listings and privacy labels; we did not audit these apps' network traffic.

If you paste a contract, a medical letter or client notes into a cloud chatbot, the text goes to a server. The apps below run the model on the phone, so a document you attach is read there. Each page says what is on-device and what is optional online, and that is what this ranking compares. Apps whose listings do not mention documents, such as Locally AI and Private LLM, are left out; our local LLM ranking covers them.

Labels and ratings checked on the US App Store on 24 November 2026. Privacy labels are declared by each developer.

1. Noema

It is built around your own files: you import PDFs and EPUBs, ask questions and get answers tied to indexed passages, and it can make summaries, notes and flashcards. Its page says local conversations and files stay on the device and the developer collects no data. Web search and remote providers are optional, so keep them off for sensitive files.

2. On-Device AI: Private Workspace

It names scanned PDFs and does the OCR on the device, only after you approve it, and keeps a private searchable library of PDFs, text and code. It also works on Mac. Online providers and web features are optional and off until you enable them, per its listing. It is the broadest of the five, and the busiest to set up.

3. Pinku

Pinku is a paid app: chatting needs Pinku Pro, a one-time $12.99 purchase or a yearly plan. You attach a PDF or text file and ask about it, and the model that answers runs on your iPhone or Mac. Per its page, there is no account and no server, chats are stored only on the device, and deleting a chat deletes the file. The network is used for model downloads and App Store purchases. The privacy label lists only Purchases and Identifiers, with no User Content.

Where others win: Noema indexes whole books and cites passages, and On-Device AI reads scanned PDFs; Pinku's listing does not mention scans or OCR. Its models are also weaker than cloud ones and have a shorter context.

Pinku is in the App Store: Pinku on the App Store. More on its page: aisagalab.com/pinku.

4. Enclave

Its listing says documents attached to a chat are processed locally, with PDFs, text files, images and source code. Pro adds cloud models, and the listing also names web search. Its label lists User Content among data that may be collected but is not linked to you, so read its settings before putting a confidential file in.

5. Privacy AI

The most capable converter of the five: Doc Scanner, a Knowledge Wiki with cited answers and conversion of PDFs, Office files and e-books with OCR. It says Smart Chat keeps sensitive text local and local models work fully offline, but it also connects to cloud providers, and its label lists identifiers and usage data that may be used to track you. We rank it last for confidential files for that reason.

What on-device does not cover

  • A document stays on the phone only while the app's online options are off. Noema, On-Device AI and Enclave all have some.
  • A privacy label is the developer's own statement. We compared labels, not traffic. For a measured example on a Mac, see what Ollama, LM Studio and Pinku sent over the network.
  • If your employer or your client has rules about software, they apply whether or not the model is local.

How to choose

  • A shelf of long PDFs and books: Noema.
  • Scanned paper: On-Device AI.
  • One PDF or text file at a time, with no online option in the app: Pinku.
  • You want local and cloud in one app and will manage the settings: Enclave.

Disclosure: we make Pinku, number 3 on this list. Everything about the other apps comes from their own App Store pages.

Can an AI app read a PDF without uploading it?

Yes, if the model runs on the phone. Noema, On-Device AI, Pinku and Enclave all say documents are handled on the device, though Noema, On-Device AI and Enclave also have optional online features to keep off.

Which app can read scanned documents on the iPhone?

On-Device AI names scanned PDFs and on-device OCR in its listing. Privacy AI also names OCR. Pinku's listing does not mention scans.

Is on-device AI safe for confidential files?

It avoids sending the text to a server, but it is not a guarantee: check the app's online options and its privacy label, and follow any rules from your employer or client.

Does Pinku send documents anywhere?

Per its page, no: there is no account and no server, and chats stay on the device. The network is used to download models and for App Store purchases.
