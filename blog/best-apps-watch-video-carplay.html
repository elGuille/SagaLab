Best apps to watch video on CarPlay while parked (2026)

Apple's own AirPlay route first, then five apps compared. Video on the car screen depends on your car and works only when you are not driving.

Maruta G · 25 November 2026 · Español

Short answer: the car decides, not the app. Video on the car screen only works in vehicles that support it, and only when you are not driving. In a supported car you have two routes: Apple's built-in AirPlay, which sends a video from the iPhone to the car display, or an app that adds what AirPlay does not: your own files, your own playlists, a media server or a browser. This page covers both and ranks five apps. We make CarScreen, number 3; see the last section. Facts about every app come from its US App Store listing, checked on 25 November 2026.

Apple's own option: AirPlay video to the car screen

Apple Support says that when you are not driving you can play videos from your iPhone on your CarPlay display, and that CarPlay video apps work in vehicles that support the video-in-car feature. To use AirPlay, connect the iPhone to CarPlay, play a video from a supported CarPlay video app or any app that supports AirPlay, tap the AirPlay button and select your CarPlay display. If your vehicle indicates that video playback is not available, the video switches to audio only.

The car has to opt in. Apple's CarPlay page for developers and automakers describes it as "AirPlay video in the car", and tells automakers to integrate support for CarPlay with AirPlay video to enable it. The Apple pages we read do not list compatible cars, so check with your car's manufacturer or dealer.

Apple's pages and outside reports differ on the iOS version, so we do not state one. What Apple's pages say is this: the support page and the developer page name no iOS version for AirPlay video to the car, and Apple's WWDC26 session on CarPlay apps says that with iOS 27 developers can create apps that browse and play videos in new cars that support the feature, and that apps that already support AirPlay video need no changes.

In Apple's iPhone User Guide, the "Play videos with CarPlay" page is in the iOS 27 edition; when we requested the iOS 26 edition address on 25 November 2026 it did not return the page. Apple's pages do not say more than that about versions.

Apple's route has one limit that matters here: it plays what an AirPlay-capable app can play. It does not read an M3U playlist, open a media server or play a folder of files for you. That is what the apps below are for.

We ranked on one question: how many kinds of your own video each app can put on the car screen, and how clearly its listing says what you need. We did not test the apps in a car. A listing is a developer's description, not a guarantee, and the car decides what shows.

1. Car Cinema: the widest list of sources

Its listing covers M3U and M3U8 playlists added by link, file, pasted text or QR code, sign-in with a TV provider's account, a Jellyfin or Emby server, a browser on the car display, screen mirroring, casting from apps that use DLNA, and clips from Photos and Files. It has no account, and its listing says playlists, favorites and history stay on the iPhone. It also says to add only playlists and sources you have the right to watch. It is the broadest in this list; the trade-off is more to set up than a single-purpose player.

2. CabinCast: videos from Files and a free hour to test your car

Its listing names MP4, MOV, MKV and AVI files from Files and Photos, M3U playlists, Jellyfin and Emby, direct links and screen mirroring. Its listing says that when the car moves the picture goes away and the sound keeps playing, and that the first hour on the car screen is free so you can check your car. It needs location access to watch on the car screen and iOS 26.0 or later.

3. CarScreen: a parked-video player that checks your car first

Our app, described in the last section. Placed third because its listing names fewer kinds of source than the first two.

4. DashTV: web video and a browser on the car screen

It is aimed at web video: you find a video on the iPhone and tap Play on Car, or browse on the car display itself, and the iPhone acts as a remote. Pro adds ad blocking on the sites you open in the app. Its listing says video and mirroring on the car screen need iOS 26.4 or later, that the car decides when video can show, and that on other cars the video plays on the iPhone instead. It does not list M3U playlists, so it fits web video better than a personal library.

5. Carvia: M3U playlists and a browser, with a different claim about cars

Its listing covers M3U, M3U8, HLS and HTTP streams, a browser on the car display where the iPhone acts as a trackpad, screen mirroring and imported video files. It states that the app opens in every CarPlay car, including cars without CarPlay video support, and that video, browser and mirroring are for passengers and parked vehicles only. Whether the video itself plays still depends on the car, as Apple's page says, so test it before paying.

How to choose

  • You only want to watch a video from an app that already supports AirPlay: Apple's own route, with no extra app.
  • Files, playlists and a media server, with the widest choice: Car Cinema.
  • Your own M3U playlists and files, with a free check of your car first: CarScreen or CabinCast.
  • Web video and browsing on the car screen: DashTV.

Whatever you choose, check your car before you pay, and never watch video while driving.

Questions people ask

Can you watch video on CarPlay while parked?

Yes, in cars that support it. Apple Support says that when you are not driving you can play a video from any app that supports AirPlay on your CarPlay display, in vehicles that support the video-in-car feature. If the car indicates video is not available, it plays as audio only.

Does every car support video on CarPlay?

No. Apple tells automakers to integrate CarPlay with AirPlay video to enable it, and the Apple pages we read do not list compatible cars. Check with the manufacturer, or use an app that tests your car first.

Can I play my own M3U playlist on CarPlay?

Yes, with a player app such as Car Cinema, CabinCast, CarScreen or Carvia, in a car that supports video. Use only playlists you have the right to play.
