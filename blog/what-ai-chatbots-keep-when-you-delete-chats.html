Are deleted AI chats really deleted? What ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and other chatbots keep (2026)

Deleting a chat removes it from your list at once. On the company's side, a copy can stay for 30 days, for years if it was reviewed or used for training, or for as long as a court says. Here is what each one says, with links.

Maruta G · 20 October 2026, updated 30 October 2026 · Español · Русский · 简体中文

The short answer

Not right away, and not always. When you delete a chat, every service below removes it from your list at once. Most then say they erase it from their servers within 30 days. Three kinds of copy can outlive that: chats kept for training (up to 5 years at Anthropic if you allowed it), chats kept for safety review (up to 3 years at Google when a human read them, 2 years at Anthropic when flagged), and chats kept because a court ordered it: from May to September 2025, OpenAI had to keep deleted ChatGPT chats for a copyright case, and it still holds what it saved then.

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

OpenAI's retention page (updated 29 October 2026) says regular and archived chats “remain saved to your account until you delete them” or a workspace retention policy removes them. When you delete one, ChatGPT removes it from view immediately and OpenAI “schedules it for permanent deletion from its systems within 30 days”, unless the chat was already de-identified and disassociated from your account, or OpenAI must keep it longer for security or legal obligations. You cannot restore a deleted chat, “in ChatGPT or through OpenAI Support”. A temporary chat is not shown in your history, but OpenAI “may keep a copy for up to 30 days for safety purposes”. Deleting a chat does not delete a file you saved separately in Library or a Space.

On training, OpenAI says that for Free, Plus and Pro it “may use your content to train our models”. Even after you opt out, if you press thumbs up or down, “the entire conversation associated with that feedback may be used to train our models”.

Settings: turn training off in Settings, Data controls, Improve the model for everyone (or Do not train on my content in the Privacy Portal). Start a temporary chat by opening a new chat and selecting Temporary. Delete one chat from its ••• menu, Delete; delete all in Settings, Data controls, Delete all chats. Export in Settings, Data controls, Export: the link can take up to 7 days and expires 24 hours after you get it, and an export cannot bring back chats you already deleted.

Claude (Anthropic)

Anthropic's retention page (1 July 2026) covers Free, Pro and Max. A deleted conversation is “removed from your chat history immediately” and “deleted from our back-end storage systems within 30 days”. The exceptions come with numbers:

  • If you allow your chats to be used to improve Claude, they are kept “in a de-identified format for up to 5 years” in training pipelines, and data already in a finished or running training run stays there.
  • Conversations flagged by its safety systems: inputs and outputs up to 2 years, and the classification scores up to 7 years.
  • Feedback you send (thumbs up or down): 5 years.
  • Incognito chats are not saved to your history and not used for training, but they are kept for 30 days by default.

Settings: training only happens if you switch on Help improve our AI models in Settings, Privacy. Start an incognito chat with the ghost icon at the top right. Delete a chat from its ⋮ menu, or tick several and use Select all. Export from Settings, Privacy on the web or desktop app (not from the iPhone or Android app); the link expires 24 hours after delivery.

Gemini (Google)

Google's Gemini Apps privacy hub (updated 26 October 2026) says your activity is auto-deleted after 18 months by default, and you can change that to 3 or 36 months, or turn auto-delete off. Temporary chats, and chats with Keep Activity off, are still kept with your account for 72 hours, and temporary chats are not used to train Google's models.

The exception that matters most: “a subset of chats are reviewed by human reviewers”, and those chats “are not deleted when you delete your activity”; they are kept for up to three years, disconnected from your account.

Settings: in Settings & help, Activity, choose Turn off or Turn off and delete activity. Start a temporary chat with Temporary chat next to New chat. Delete one item with Delete activity item, or everything with Delete, All time. Export with Google Takeout (My Activity, Gemini Apps); the email link stays active for 7 days.

Microsoft Copilot

Microsoft's consumer Copilot privacy FAQ says: “By default, we store conversation activity for 18 months. You can delete individual conversations or your entire conversation history at any time.” Since we first read it, Microsoft has added a line saying that the article “applies only to the older version of the Microsoft Copilot app”. The privacy page for the new app does not give a retention period, so we cannot tell you how long the new app keeps chats. Some conversations go through automated and human review, and you “can opt out of use of your conversation activity for model training at any time”.

Settings (older app): Privacy, Training on conversation activity and Training on voice conversations. Delete one conversation from the three dots next to it, or all of them with Delete all activity history. We did not find a temporary chat mode on Microsoft's pages.

Perplexity, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral and Meta AI

  • Perplexity keeps personal information “as long as your account is active”. If you delete the account, it is removed from its servers within 30 days, and signing back in before then cancels the deletion. You can delete threads one by one or all at once (the … next to the threads tab, Delete sessions); uploaded files are kept for 30 days by default. Content from Memory may be used to improve its models unless you turn off AI Data Retention in Settings, Preferences, Artificial Intelligence, and a log of cleared memories may be kept for up to 30 days.
  • Grok (xAI, now SpaceXSI; policy of 27 October 2026): Private Chat conversations, deleted conversations and deleted accounts are erased within 30 days “unless it is necessary to retain the data for legal, compliance, or safety purposes”. Training is one of the listed uses of your content. Its FAQ gives the switches: Settings, Data Controls, Improve the model; the ghost icon at the top right for a Private Chat; and Settings, Data Controls, Delete All Conversations.
  • DeepSeek (policy of 10 February 2026) keeps personal data for as long as necessary, uses it to train and improve its technology, gives a right to object to that, and stores data on servers in the People's Republic of China. You can delete your chat history in settings; deleting the account cannot be undone, and no deletion timeline is given. DeepSeek has no help pages with the steps.
  • Vibe by Mistral (formerly Le Chat): conversations “are removed from our systems upon manual deletion” or when you delete your account, typically after a short grace period, with retention for monitoring and legal purposes. Users “are not opted out by default” of training: on the phone the switch is Enable data sharing; on the web, Allow your interactions to be used to train our models.
  • Meta AI: Settings, Data & privacy, Manage your information, Delete all chats and media; public vibes are not deleted with them. The same page says Meta uses your interactions with its AIs “to improve AI at Meta”, and that when its AIs cannot answer, Meta shares your messages and general information such as your region with select partners. You can export your information from Accounts Center. We did not find a retention period for chats.

Four times chats were kept or exposed anyway

A court ordered OpenAI to keep deleted chats (2025)

In the New York Times copyright case, Magistrate Judge Ona Wang ordered OpenAI on 13 May 2025 to “preserve and segregate all output log data that would otherwise be deleted”, “whether such data might be deleted at a user's request” or because of privacy laws. OpenAI says this covered ChatGPT Free, Plus, Pro and Team and the API without a zero data retention agreement, not Enterprise or Edu. A stipulated order of 9 October 2025 ended the obligation “as of September 26, 2025”. OpenAI keeps what it had already saved, except data from the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the United Kingdom, and keeps preserving chats from accounts linked to the domains the newspapers listed. OpenAI's own page says it “will securely store limited historical April–September 2025 user data”. The case is still open; we found no change to the preservation terms since.

Italy limited ChatGPT and fined OpenAI (2023 and 2024)

On 30 March 2023, Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, ordered an immediate temporary limitation of ChatGPT's processing of data of people in Italy, and on 11 April set the conditions for lifting it, to be met by 30 April 2023. On 20 December 2024 it fined OpenAI fifteen million euros, for reasons that included not reporting the March 2023 breach, training without a legal basis, a lack of transparency and no age checks. The Garante's page now says that, after the Court of Rome upheld OpenAI's appeal in a judgment published on 18 March 2026, the decision has been temporarily taken off its website.

A ChatGPT bug showed other people's chat titles (March 2023)

OpenAI wrote that a bug “allowed some users to see titles from another active user's chat history”, and exposed payment-related information of 1.2% of ChatGPT Plus subscribers active during a nine-hour window; full card numbers were not exposed.

A DeepSeek database was open to the internet (January 2025)

Wiz Research found a DeepSeek database reachable without a password, with “over a million lines of log streams containing chat history, secret keys, backend details”; DeepSeek closed it promptly after being told. The next day, 30 January 2025, the Garante ordered an urgent limitation of DeepSeek's processing of data of users in Italy.

Never paste passwords, API keys or card numbers

The companies say it themselves. OpenAI: “Please don't share any sensitive information in your conversations.” Google: “Please don't enter confidential information that you wouldn't want a reviewer to see or Google to use to improve our services”, and “Don't enter login or payment information into Gemini chats”. Microsoft: you “shouldn't provide any confidential or sensitive personal data”. xAI: “please do not share any personal or sensitive information in your questions to Grok.” The UK's National Cyber Security Centre recommends “not to include sensitive information in queries to public LLMs”.

The reasons are in the sections above: a deleted chat can stay up to 30 days, a reviewed one up to three years, a court can freeze deletion, and a leak exposes whatever is in the logs. If you have already pasted a password or a key into a chatbot, deleting the chat is not enough: change the password or revoke the key.

Chatbots popular in Russian and Chinese

  • Alice (Yandex): without the paid option, chat history is kept for 14 days; with Alice Plus, without a time limit. You delete a chat from the history, and data by category from Yandex ID's data management page, which warns that some data is kept longer “because the law requires it”. Yandex ID also lets you order an archive of your data.
  • GigaChat (Sber): the agreement we read (6 February 2026) does not give a retention period for conversations.
  • Doubao (ByteDance): keeps personal information only for the period necessary to provide the service and stores data collected in China inside China. You can delete single conversations and the account. The policy says it uses your inputs and replies to improve its models, and a switch in privacy settings turns that off. It lists rights to view, copy and delete, without an export button.
  • Kimi (Moonshot AI): stores personal information in China. You can delete conversations and the account. There is no training switch: you contact support, and after verifying your identity they stop using your content for training.

What a formal request got me

On 15 April 2026 I sent OpenAI and Anthropic a formal GDPR request from my own consumer accounts, asking under Article 15 for everything they held, including safety flags and data derived from my chats, and under Article 17 for erasure, with written confirmation of what was deleted and the legal basis for anything kept. Anthropic acknowledged it automatically the same day. OpenAI replied on 17 April; Anthropic's human reply arrived on 16 May, one month and one day later. The GDPR gives one month to respond (Article 12(3)).

Both replies were polite and pointed to self-service tools. Neither confirmed in writing what had been deleted, neither gave a legal basis for what was kept, and neither answered the access request with the data itself. I did not take it to a data protection authority, so nothing has been decided about it. The full exchange, quoted, is in I sent OpenAI and Anthropic a GDPR erasure request.

How to keep a conversation out of all of this

  1. Before typing, turn off training (ChatGPT, Copilot, Grok, Mistral, Perplexity's Memory, Doubao) or leave it off (Claude). Do not press thumbs up or down on chats you care about: on ChatGPT, Claude and Mistral, feedback has its own rules.
  2. Use temporary or incognito chats for sensitive questions, and remember they are still kept for a while: up to 30 days on ChatGPT, Claude and Grok, 72 hours on Gemini.
  3. Delete what you do not need, and export first if you want a copy: an export cannot bring back deleted chats.
  4. Keep secrets out of every chat: passwords, API keys, card and ID numbers.

When a model on your own device makes sense

None of the cloud chatbots above promise to keep nothing: deletion takes time, there are exceptions, and a court or a leak can override both. A model that runs on your own device avoids that for the conversations where it matters (health, money, work documents, anything you would not want a reviewer to read), because the text never reaches a server. The trade-off is a smaller model: it knows less, makes more mistakes and cannot search the web, so it suits private drafting, summaries and questions, not research.

On a computer, tools such as Ollama or LM Studio run open models; on an iPhone or Mac, apps such as Locally AI, PocketPal AI and Private LLM do, and so does Pinku, which we make. Pinku keeps chats on the device: they never leave it. Apple's privacy label for Pinku lists only purchase history and an anonymous ID, used for Pinku Pro. Questions people ask

Are deleted ChatGPT chats really deleted?

They disappear from your account at once and cannot be restored. OpenAI says it deletes them from its systems within 30 days, unless they were already de-identified or it must keep them for security or legal obligations. Deleted chats saved under the court order of May to September 2025 are still kept.

Does OpenAI still have to keep my deleted ChatGPT chats?

Not new ones. The obligation ended on 26 September 2025. OpenAI keeps what it had saved before that date, except data from the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the United Kingdom, and keeps preserving chats of accounts linked to domains the New York Times listed.

How long does Claude keep my chats after I delete them?

Anthropic says a deleted chat leaves your history immediately and its back-end storage within 30 days. Chats flagged by safety systems are kept up to 2 years (scores up to 7 years), and chats used for training with your permission up to 5 years, de-identified.

Does deleting Gemini activity delete chats that a human reviewed?

No. Google says reviewed chats are not deleted when you delete your activity; they are kept for up to three years, disconnected from your account.

Is a temporary or incognito chat stored anywhere?

Yes, for a while: up to 30 days on ChatGPT and Grok, 30 days by default on Claude, and 72 hours on Gemini.

Is it safe to paste a password or API key into ChatGPT?

No. OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and xAI all ask you not to share sensitive information, and deleted chats can stay on their servers for weeks or longer. If you already did, change the password or revoke the key.

Which AI chat does not store my conversations at all?

None of the cloud chatbots in this list promise that. A model that runs on your own device, such as Ollama or LM Studio on a computer, or Locally AI, PocketPal AI, Private LLM or Pinku on iPhone or Mac, keeps the conversation on the device.
