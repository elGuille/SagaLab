Best apps to hide photos on Android (2026)

Five vault apps compared, plus the three folders that come with Android phones. The real choice is whether you need video, a decoy PIN or a cloud copy, or want nothing to leave the phone.

Maruta G · 25 November 2026 · Español

Short answer: there is no single best app. LockMyPix has the longest feature list on its Google Play page, Keepsafe is the choice if you want a cloud copy, and Photo Vault, which we make, is a fit if you only keep photos, want no account and want nothing to leave the phone. Photo Vault does not handle video and has no decoy PIN or disguised icon on Android, so it is third below. If you only need to hide a few photos, the folders already on your phone may be enough.

We compare each app on what its own Google Play listing says: price model, photos and video, cloud copy, decoy PIN or fake vault, disguised icon. We rank on one question: which app keeps private photos on the phone, behind a lock, with the most useful features. Details were checked on 25 November 2026 against the listings, and a Not listed cell means the listing does not mention it, not that the feature is certainly missing. Data safety labels are what each developer declares to Google Play, not something we have tested. We make one of the apps, Photo Vault; see the section from our team at the end.

First, the folders that come with your phone

Three options need no extra app, and each has limits:

  • Google Photos Locked Folder. Needs Android 6 or later and uses your device screen lock as its password. Items you do not back up are stored only on that device and can be lost if you uninstall Photos, clear its data or factory reset, according to Google's help page.
  • Samsung Secure Folder (Galaxy phones). Needs a Samsung account and a lock you choose, and Samsung says it encrypts what is stored there. Our Samsung Galaxy guide has the steps and limits.
  • Files by Google Safe folder. Android 8.0 and later, locked with a PIN or pattern. Google warns that if you forget the PIN or pattern you cannot access the files, and Back up to Google Drive is not available in it.

1. LockMyPix: most features

The free edition's listing says it uses AES CTR encryption on each file, and offers PIN, fingerprint, password, pattern or face login, albums and sub-albums, SD card support, a decoy vault with a separate code, an intruder selfie and a disguised icon (Calculator or Radio). The decoy vault, disguise, intruder selfie and face unlock are listed as Premium. Its Data safety label declares that it may collect personal info and app info and may share device IDs. It does not mention a cloud copy. Choose it if you want the longest list of security features and are happy to pay for Premium.

2. Keepsafe: best if you want a cloud copy

The listing describes a Private Cloud for up to 10,000 items that restores your photos if the phone is lost, a face-down auto lock, and, in Premium, album PINs, break-in alerts and a Fake Pin that opens a decoy vault. Its Data safety label declares that it may share personal info and financial info, among other types, and may collect location and personal info, among others. That is the trade-off for the cloud: your photos leave the device.

3. Photo Vault: photos only, nothing leaves the phone

Our Android app keeps photos in a private encrypted space inside the app. Its Google Play listing says there are no accounts, no cloud, no ads and no analytics, and the Data safety label declares no data collected and none shared. You pick photos with the Android system Photo Picker, so the app does not ask for access to your whole gallery. Each photo is encrypted on the device with the Tink library and a key held in the Android Keystore. You unlock with a PIN of 4 to 8 digits, or with the fingerprint or face unlock already set on the phone. It organises photos into albums, blocks screenshots inside the app and hides its content in the recent apps screen. It can offer to remove the originals from your gallery after import.

Where the others beat it: it supports images only, not video. It has no decoy PIN, no disguised icon, no album passwords and no intruder photo. There is no cloud backup and no backup file: the listing says that if you uninstall the app, clear its data or change phones, the vault is permanently deleted, and a forgotten PIN cannot be recovered. The free plan has limits on photos and albums, and Photo Vault Pro removes them. It is a new app on Android. About Photo Vault (the iPhone version has more features than the Android one).

4. GalleryVault: files and a hidden icon

It hides photos, videos and other files, encrypts them, can hide its own icon, has a fake passcode, break-in alerts and a shake-to-close option, and its FAQ says files are stored only on your device, so you back them up yourself before changing phones. The listing says it uses the Device Administrator permission, and its Data safety label declares that it may share app activity, app info and performance, and device or other IDs.

5. Hide It Pro: free, with a cloud backup

The listing calls it completely free and unlimited. It disguises itself as Audio Manager, hides photos, videos, messages and apps, offers an escape PIN and a free cloud backup, and has a built-in encryption tool. The Data safety label declares that it may share location and device or other IDs. Its last update on Google Play was 4 September 2026, so check how current it is before you rely on it.

How to choose

  • A few photos on a Samsung or Pixel phone: Secure Folder or Locked Folder, with the backup limits above.
  • Video, a decoy and a disguised icon: LockMyPix or GalleryVault.
  • A cloud copy so the vault survives a lost phone: Keepsafe or Hide It Pro, knowing your photos then leave the device.
  • Photos only, no account and nothing leaving the phone: Photo Vault on Google Play (iPhone: App Store (Google Play)).

Questions people ask

What is the best app to hide photos on Android?

It depends on what you need. LockMyPix has the longest feature list on its Google Play page, Keepsafe adds a cloud copy, and Photo Vault, which we make, keeps photos only on the phone with no account but does not support video. For a few photos, Google Photos Locked Folder or Samsung Secure Folder may be enough.

Does Android have a built-in way to hide photos?

Yes. Google Photos has Locked Folder (Android 6 or later), Samsung Galaxy phones have Secure Folder, and Files by Google has a Safe folder (Android 8.0 or later). Each has limits, mainly around backup and moving to a new phone.

Does a photo vault app back up my photos?

It depends on the app. Keepsafe and Hide It Pro list a cloud copy. GalleryVault says files stay only on the device. Photo Vault for Android has no cloud backup, so uninstalling it or changing phones deletes the vault.

Does Photo Vault for Android support videos?

No. Its Google Play listing says it supports images and that videos are not supported.

Do hidden-photo apps remove the photo from my gallery?

Usually you import the photo into the app and then delete the original. Photo Vault offers to remove the originals after import; on Android 11 and newer, Android shows its own confirmation dialog and you decide.
