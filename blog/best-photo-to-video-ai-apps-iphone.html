Best photo-to-video AI apps on iPhone for your own and family photos (2026)

A family photo is not a product shot. Here is which apps are built for it, and what each one's privacy label says about the pictures you give it.

Maruta G · 2 November 2026 · Leer en español

Everything here comes from App Store pages, privacy labels and help pages read on 2 November 2026. We did not test video quality, so the order is by how directly each app serves this job, not by which looks best.

Most photo-to-video rankings judge effects and models. With your own photos, and above all with photos of relatives, two other questions come first: is the app made for this kind of picture, and where do the pictures go? Read the label column before you upload anything.

The label is what each developer declares to Apple, not an audit. “User Content” is Apple's category for things such as photos and videos.

1. MyHeritage: made for family photos

It is a family tree app first. Its page says Deep Nostalgia makes the faces in historical family photos move, and LiveMemory turns photos into video clips and can animate entire family photos. It also repairs, colorizes and scans old photos in the same app. Choose it for grandparents' pictures, especially if you want the tree too. The app is much bigger than this one job, and it charges through subscriptions.

2. Google Photos: if your photos are already there

Its help page says Photo to video needs the photo to be backed up, needs a connection and a personal Google Account, and requires you to be at least 13, or 18 for the text prompt option. You get a limited number of generations a day, more with a Google AI plan, and Google warns of “unexpected or inaccurate results”. It also says these features are not available in all regions. If your library is not in Google Photos, using this means putting it there first.

3. Kling AI: when the clip matters more than the memory

A general generator. Its page promises up to 15 seconds in native 1080p or 4K, and extension up to 3 minutes. Nothing on the page is aimed at family photos. It also has a community feed, so check what you share. If it does not fit, see these Kling AI alternatives for iPhone.

4. Dora: a photo of you, a pet or a product

You upload a photo and pick a template or describe what should happen, then get the video in about two minutes. Its page says the cost is shown before you generate, the coins come back if a generation fails, and videos have no watermark. Its account also works on the web and inside Claude and ChatGPT. One limit matters here: its page says it does not create content involving minors, so it is not for photos of your children. It needs a paid plan, and it has far fewer ratings than the rest of this list.

5. Runway: several models in one app

Its page says it turns any photo into motion and lists models such as Seedance 2.5, Kling 3 Pro and Gen-4.5, with a first generation in about two minutes. Characters can stay consistent across shots, and work syncs with your computer. It suits someone who wants to compare and make a short film more than someone animating one picture. Other options: Runway alternatives for iPhone.

6. Motionleap: move part of the photo

You choose what moves in the picture: water, clouds, a candle flame, steam from a cup. Its page is about moving elements in still photos, not about people, so it suits a holiday or landscape picture. Its label is the only one here that does not list user content.

Before you upload a photo of someone else

  • Ask them if you can. It is their face, and the app's servers may keep it for a time set by its own privacy policy.
  • Keep the original. An AI video is a new interpretation, not a restored copy: expressions and movements are invented.
  • Read the label and the privacy policy linked from the App Store page. Our table covers only the label.
  • Do not present the result as real footage. Say it was made with AI when you share it.

Who we left out

Apps built mainly around jokes and viral effects, such as Revive and Pika, do animate photos (here are some Pika alternatives), but we kept this list to apps where a family picture is treated as more than a meme. Questions people ask

What is the best app to animate old family photos on iPhone?

MyHeritage is the one on this list built for it: its page describes Deep Nostalgia for historical family photos and LiveMemory for video clips. We did not test quality.

Can I animate photos with Google Photos on iPhone?

Google's help page describes Photo to video, for backed-up photos, with a limited number of generations a day and not in all regions.

Can I use an AI video app with photos of my children?

It depends on the app. Dora's page says it does not create content involving minors. Google Photos sets a minimum age for the account holder, 13. Check each app's own rules, and think about whether the picture should leave your phone at all.

Do these apps keep my photos?

We can only say what the labels declare. MyHeritage, Google Photos and Kling AI list user content as linked to you, at least in part. Dora and Runway list it as not linked. Motionleap does not list it. How long a photo is kept is set by each privacy policy.

Are these apps free?

All can be downloaded for free and sell in-app purchases. Runway says it is free to start. Google Photos gives a limited number of generations a day. Dora needs a paid plan.
