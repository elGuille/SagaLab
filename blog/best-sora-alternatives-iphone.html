Best Sora alternatives for iPhone (2026)

OpenAI closed Sora in 2026. Six AI video apps for iPhone to use instead, and what each one is best at. One of them is ours.

Maruta G · 25 November 2026 · Español

Short answer: OpenAI closed the Sora app and website on 26 April 2026 and the Sora API on 24 September 2026 (OpenAI Help Center). On iPhone the closest replacements are apps that put several video models in one place: Runway lists Seedance 2.5, Kling 3 Pro and Gen-4.5, and Kling AI runs its own model with clips up to 30 seconds in 1080p or 4K. Luma Dream Machine is the one built around keeping the same person across scenes. Dora is ours: it is made for short vertical clips from a photo or a description. Details are from the US App Store listings on 25 November 2026.

No app here is Sora, and none of them claim to be. They differ in which models they run and what you do after the clip is made.

1. Runway: many models, one app

The listing says "every model, one app" and names Seedance 2.5, Kling 3 Pro, Gen-4.5 and image models. It covers text to video, image to video and video to video, and a Runway Agent that plans the shots and assembles the video from a conversation.

Where others beat it: it is built for choosing among models, which is more to learn than a template app. Luma Dream Machine is more direct for keeping one character across scenes.

2. Kling AI: longer clips

Kling's listing describes text and image to video with up to 30 seconds in native 1080p or 4K, extension to videos up to 3 minutes, image generation and a community where you can clone a work and try the idea.

Where others beat it: the app is a creative platform with a lot in it. For a quick vertical clip from one photo, a template app such as Dora has fewer steps.

3. Luma Dream Machine: the same person in every scene

Luma's listing says you can reference a person with just one face photo and create varied images and videos of them, instruct with up to four images, and use Modify to edit by describing your changes.

Where others beat it: its listing does not mention templates for ready-made trends, which PixVerse Lite and Dora list.

4. Dora: short clips from a photo, made for social

Dora makes short AI videos and images on iPhone: pick a template or write an idea, add a photo, and the listing says a video comes back in about two minutes. It offers vertical 9:16, square or horizontal, up to 1080p, no watermark, Motion Control to copy a dance onto the person in your photo, and images up to 4K. The cost in coins is shown before you generate, and failed generations are refunded. The same account works on the web and inside Claude through its connector. It is on iPhone and Android. We make Dora.

Where others beat it: it does not offer the model choice of Runway, the 30-second and 4K clips Kling lists, or Luma's character references. You pay in coins, so check the cost on screen before you tap.

5. Pika: effects and playful edits

Pika's listing is about effects: Pikaffects such as melt, explode or squish, Pikadditions to drop objects or people into your clips, Pikaswaps to replace an element, and Pikascenes.

Where others beat it: it leans toward effects and memes, so for a straight cinematic scene look at Runway or Kling AI.

6. PixVerse Lite: trends and controls

PixVerse Lite runs PixVerse V6 plus other models and lists text to video, image to video, Modify Video, Motion Control, first and last frame transitions, endless video extension and upscaling up to 4K.

Where others beat it: the long feature list is a lot for a first clip, and its listing does not highlight a single-person reference the way Luma's does.

How to choose

  • The widest choice of models: Runway.
  • Longer or 4K clips: Kling AI.
  • The same character across scenes: Luma Dream Machine.
  • A fast vertical clip from a photo, with the cost shown first: Dora.
  • Playful effects: Pika. Motion and transitions: PixVerse Lite.
