How to protect photos of your ID, passport and cards on iPhone (2026)

A photo of your passport sitting in the camera roll is in every place your camera roll goes. Decide where it should live, move it there, and delete the copies you forgot about.

Maruta G · 23 October 2026 · Leer en español

We make Photo Vault, one of the options on this page. The built-in options come first, and for many people they are enough.

Where that photo actually is

When you take a picture of your ID to send to a landlord, a hotel or a bank, it lands in the Photos library like any other photo. That means:

  • Anyone who opens your unlocked phone can scroll to it, and it can show up in Memories, search and the Photos widget.
  • With iCloud Photos on, it is in iCloud too. Apple's iCloud security overview (Apple, 102651) lists Photos among the data that is end-to-end encrypted only when you turn on Advanced Data Protection. With the default, standard data protection, the encryption keys are kept in Apple's data centres.
  • Apps you gave full photo access can read your whole library, not only the photos you picked.

What we checked in Photo Vault's code

We read the storage code of the version on sale today (1.2.10). Every file in the vault gets the iOS Data Protection class completeUnlessOpen, which means the file cannot be read while the iPhone is locked, and the vault folders are marked excluded from backup, so they do not go into regular iCloud or computer backups. Both settings are in MediaFileManager.swift and covered by the app's tests. The flip side: without Photo Vault's own backup, the photos are lost with the phone.

Step by step

  1. Find every copy. In Photos, search for the document type (passport, ID, card) and scroll Recents around the date you sent it. Check Messages, Mail and WhatsApp threads where you sent it, and the Downloads folder in Files.
  2. Keep only the ones you need. An expired passport or a card you cancelled does not need a photo. Delete those rather than hide them.
  3. Choose where the rest live. Hidden album for the least effort, a locked note if you also keep notes about the document, or a vault app if you want it out of the library and out of iCloud Photos.
  4. Move them, then delete the originals. If you use a vault such as Photo Vault, import the photos, then delete them in Photos: importing makes a copy. The same applies to a note.
  5. Empty Recently Deleted. Deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted for up to 30 days (Apple). Open it and delete them there too.
  6. Next time, take the photo inside the vault. Photo Vault has its own camera; a photo taken there never touches the camera roll.
  7. Mark what you send. When you must send a copy, use Markup in Photos to write who it is for and the date across it. A marked copy is less useful to anyone else who gets hold of it.
  8. Limit photo access. In Settings, Privacy & Security, Photos, set apps that do not need your whole library to Limited Access.

Where Photo Vault fits, and where it does not

Photo Vault keeps photos and videos on the iPhone behind Face ID, Touch ID or a passcode, with an optional password per album, so an ID album can stay closed when you open the vault for someone. It can show a different, harmless set of photos with a decoy code (Premium), change its home screen icon to Calculator, Notes or Weather, and there is no account. Backups are either an exported file locked with a password you choose, or its optional iCloud copy (Premium), end-to-end encrypted in your own iCloud.

It does not scan documents or import PDFs, it is iPhone only, and it relies on iOS Data Protection rather than its own per-file encryption. If you already use a password manager that keeps attachments, that may be the better home for ID scans. How Photo Vault protects your files.

Questions people ask

Is it safe to keep a photo of my passport on my iPhone?

It is as safe as the places that photo reaches. In the camera roll it is visible to anyone using your unlocked phone, to apps with full photo access and, with iCloud Photos, in iCloud. Keep only what you need and move it somewhere locked.

Are iCloud Photos end-to-end encrypted?

Only with Advanced Data Protection turned on. Apple's iCloud security overview says that with standard data protection, the default, the keys are kept in Apple's data centres.

Is the Hidden album enough for ID photos?

It hides them from the library and locks them with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, but they stay in your library and in iCloud Photos, and anyone with your passcode can open them.

Does Photo Vault upload my ID photos?

No, unless you turn on its optional iCloud copy (Premium), which is end-to-end encrypted in your own iCloud. The vault is excluded from regular backups and has no account or server.

Should I send a photo of my ID by WhatsApp or email?

Only when you have to. Mark the copy with who it is for and the date, and delete it from the chat and your camera roll afterwards.
